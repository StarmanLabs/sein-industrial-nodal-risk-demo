# Illustrative Mining Screening Case: TINTAYA EXISTENTE 138

## Decision question

What would justify opening a deeper electricity due-diligence review for a hypothetical continuous-load mining operation considering a contractual or connection reference related to `TINTAYA EXISTENTE 138`?

This is an illustrative workflow. It does not represent a named new mining project and does not recommend a location.

## What is observed

| Field | Observed value |
|---|---:|
| Barra | `TINTAYA EXISTENTE 138` |
| Voltage | 138 kV |
| Analytical coverage | 36 of 36 months, 2023-01 to 2025-12 |
| Average relative nodal stress | 71.42 |
| Average operational priority | 63.13 |
| Operational-priority P90 | 90.59 |
| Product review score | 73.64 |
| Priority / monthly-follow-up months | 9 / 19 |
| Result-stability score | 97.28 |
| Weight-sensitivity rank range | 7 to 8 across seven scenarios |

The reviewed context identifies `TINTAYA` as a 138 kV transmission-substation context. Official-source review also records the Tintaya Nueva-Tintaya Existente 138 kV line context. This supports a topology-aware evidence request, not a claim about available capacity, direction of flow or congestion.

## What is assumed

The Industrial Exposure page contains a `continuous-load mining` archetype with **50,000 MWh per month assumed consumption**. This is a scenario parameter, not observed demand for a mine.

For `TINTAYA EXISTENTE 138`, the scenario produces these relative exposure scores:

| Contract archetype | Assumed spot share | Average relative exposure score | P90 score | Follow-up months |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reference fixed PPA | 0% | 41.16 | 50.76 | 0 |

These differences show how the review priority changes under explicit exposure assumptions. They are not bill forecasts and should not be interpreted as actual contract savings or losses.

## How an analyst uses the dashboard

1. Open **Industrial Exposure**, select `Continuous-load mining`, and compare contract archetypes.
2. Check whether `TINTAYA EXISTENTE 138` remains relevant and identify the assumed exposure driver.
3. Open **Barra Priority Ranking** and review its score, category and result stability.
4. Open the [barra case directly](https://sein-industrial-nodal-risk-demo-edaw4ndep4pm9plvudnyov.streamlit.app/?page=Caso%20de%20Estudio&barra=TINTAYA+EXISTENTE+138) to inspect monthly recurrence and reviewed context.
5. Convert the screening result into a request for project-specific evidence.

## What the current project cannot answer

The local project does not contain a complete, decision-ready layer for plant-level thermal and hydro generation, reservoir hydrology, hourly dispatch, transmission capacity, flows, contingencies or connection costs. Therefore it cannot determine whether a thermal or hydroelectric plant physically supplies the proposed operation, attribute the marginal-price signal to one generator, or select an optimal connection point.

## Evidence required before a real siting decision

- Project coordinates and technically feasible connection alternatives.
- Contract and settlement barra, PPA/indexation terms, pass-throughs, losses, power charges, tolls and taxes.
- Hourly load curve, maximum demand, ramping, expansion stages and operational flexibility.
- Hourly generation by unit and fuel, unit availability and outage history.
- Hydrological inflows, reservoir levels and water constraints for relevant hydroelectric plants.
- Validated network studies: access, load flow, short circuit, quality, reliability and contingencies.
- Connection CAPEX/OPEX, easements, permits, schedule and execution risk.
- Project-specific economic comparison under consistent production, contract and system scenarios.

## Decision enabled

The current product can justify **where to start investigating and what to request next**. It cannot close the location, engineering, contract or investment decision.
